The Statistical History of NVIDIA (2020–2026)

Phase 1 (2020): +143% – Early pre‑pandemic cycle, traditional GPU demand.

Phase 2 (2020–2021): +243% – Global liquidity bubble & crypto/gaming expansion.

Phase 3 (2021–2022): −68.25% – Structural rupture (Chow Test failure). Complete cycle reset.

Phase 4 (2022–2023): +216.23% – Regime shift driven by generative AI.

Phase 5 (2023–2024): +220.87% – Hyperbolic acceleration from Data Center demand.

Phase 6 (Mid 2024): −40% – Institutional profit‑taking, technical over‑extension correction.

Phase 7 (2024–2025): +124% – Maturation of the secular AI rally.

Phase 8 (2025): −20% – Minor volatility compression / EMA fan support.

Phase 9 (2025–2026): +40% – Active trend impulse toward $234.14 – $236.51.

Three key statistical conclusions:

  • Asymmetric drawdown compression: Sequential drawdowns (−68% → −40% → −20%) show NVIDIA transitioning from a high‑beta speculative stock to essential infrastructure in institutional portfolios.
  • Logistic S‑curve (Base Effect): Expansive impulses are decelerating (+243% → +220% → +124% → +40%). As market cap exceeds $3–$4 trillion, larger liquidity mass is required to generate multiplicative returns.
  • EMA Fan alignment: The structure EMA20 > EMA40 > EMA80 > EMA200 confirms that recent corrections do not even breach the EMA 200, systematically bouncing off higher dynamic supports.

Market Catalysts & Defensive Moat:

  • Hugging Face acquisition ($12,930M): Controls the global open‑source platform, locking developers into NVIDIA’s CUDA/GPU architecture.
  • Supply chain securing ($279,000M): Long‑term HBM memory and advanced packaging commitments to block production capacity from competitors.
  • Architecture evolution: Successful transition from Hopper → Blackwell → Vera Rubin, lifting projected revenue per GPU generation.
  • Custom chips expansion: $3,500M convertible bond investment in MediaTek for NVLink Fusion integration.

Risks identified by Wall Street:

  • Circular financing: NVIDIA has invested in OpenAI, CoreWeave, and SpaceX, and these companies use the money to buy NVIDIA chips.
  • Depreciation of chips: Hyperscalers depreciate chips in 4–6 years, but some experts estimate a useful life of only 2–3 years.
  • China risk: NVIDIA has excluded Data Center revenue from China from its guidance.
